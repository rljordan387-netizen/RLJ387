A Workday transformation can appear healthy while the conditions required for success are weakening. Milestones are being completed. Design sessions are occurring. Status reports remain reassuring. Yet critical decisions are aging, data ownership is unclear, testing evidence is incomplete, and the business is not prepared to operate differently.

The executive sponsor cannot manage every detail. The sponsor must, however, know whether the program is producing evidence of readiness or simply evidence of activity.

## Workday is an enterprise transformation

Workday affects how people are hired, paid, developed, supported, and managed. It also changes how leaders receive information and how teams make decisions. Treating the program as a technology installation narrows the definition of success before the work begins.

The platform can be configured correctly while the enterprise remains unprepared. A successful transformation requires the operating model, data, roles, processes, controls, and employee experience to move together.

## Reported status is not the same as program health

Status reporting summarizes what the program believes about itself. Program health is demonstrated through evidence.

An executive sponsor should be able to see whether:

- Critical decisions have named owners and firm resolution dates
- Requirements trace through design, build, and testing
- Data has been converted at realistic volume and reconciled
- Defects are declining at a rate consistent with the schedule
- Business readiness is measured by role and ability to perform
- Cutover has been rehearsed under realistic conditions
- The system integrator's claims align with the underlying artifacts

When these signals are unavailable, the absence is itself meaningful.

## Five questions sponsors should ask

### 1. What is the transformation intended to change?

The answer should extend beyond replacing a system. Leaders should be able to name the operating outcomes, workforce experiences, decision improvements, and business capabilities the program is expected to create.

### 2. Where is the program relying on assumption rather than evidence?

Every major claim of readiness should be supported by an artifact, result, decision, or observable behavior. Confidence without evidence is not assurance.

### 3. Which decisions are aging, and what are they blocking?

Unresolved decisions create hidden queues across design, build, data, testing, and readiness. The issue is rarely the number of open decisions. It is whether the important ones are preventing coordinated movement.

### 4. Is the business prepared to operate differently on day one?

Training completion is useful, but it does not prove readiness. Sponsors need to know whether process owners, managers, support teams, and employees can perform their responsibilities in the new environment.

### 5. What would make us delay go live?

A credible program defines its go live criteria before schedule pressure peaks. If leaders cannot describe the evidence that would cause a delay, the decision is being governed by the date rather than readiness.

## Independence improves the quality of the decision

The people delivering a transformation are essential to its success, but proximity creates pressure. Teams naturally defend their plans, protect momentum, and interpret ambiguous evidence optimistically.

An independent assessment does not replace program leadership or the system integrator. It gives executive sponsors a different vantage point: one centered on evidence, cross functional risk, and the enterprise's ability to absorb the transformation.

> Executive sponsorship is not sustained by receiving more status. It is strengthened by seeing the evidence required to make informed decisions.

The earlier sponsors establish that evidence based view, the more options they retain. Risks can be resolved while there is still time to change course, strengthen ownership, and protect the outcomes the Workday transformation was intended to deliver.
